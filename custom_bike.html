Custom Carbon Fiber Bike Build

Designing, simulating, and building a carbon fiber bike frame (work in progress)

Overview

An ongoing project to design and build my own carbon fiber road bike frame from scratch. The frame was modeled entirely in NX and simulated with NX FEA — first as isotropic aluminum, with NX Laminate simulations for the composite layup to follow.

The build follows a foam-core approach: an old Specialized Allez serves as the geometry reference and is clamped into a plywood-and-2×4 jig, foam tubes are mitered and assembled in that same jig around a metal head tube and bottom bracket shell, and the composite is then wrapped over the foam tube by tube. The foam frame is complete and the first carbon layers are on; the photos below follow the build so far, and I'll keep adding to them as it progresses.

Build log

The build is underway. An old Specialized Allez is the reference for the geometry: it gets stripped and clamped into a simple plywood-and-2×4 jig so the head tube, bottom bracket, seat tube, and dropouts are all held in the right relationship, then the frame comes out and the new tubes are built up in the same fixture.

Left: the donor frame and a stack of foam tube blanks on the jig base. Right: the Allez clamped in the jig, which locates the head tube, bottom bracket shell, and rear dropouts.

Foam tubes mitered and tacked into place one at a time in the jig, until the full front and rear triangles are standing on their own.

Head tube end of the foam frame; the metal head tube and bottom bracket shell are bonded in so the composite wraps around real bearing surfaces.

First carbon layers going on tube by tube — the top tube and seat tube wrapped, with the rest of the frame still in foam.
